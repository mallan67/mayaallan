## 0. Thesis — own the after-session dataset

Nobody measures what happens after the session.

- Oregon publishes a 470-column data file each quarter. Zero column names mention integration, follow-up, aftercare or support. The only post-session field counts reactions (E2).
- The largest real-world study of the Oregon program (JAMA Network Open, 2026-08-19, n=346, 24 centers) was covered by 8+ outlets. The coverage we read says nothing about aftercare (E7, E9).
- At the FDA hearing reported 2026-09-15, stakeholders asked for standard, interoperable data, and one said "Integration gives it durability" (E10).
- The best-known independent drug survey shows nothing newer than 2024 (E12). The leading psychedelic data site tracks policy, pipeline and Oregon volumes, but not aftercare (E13).
- Meanwhile 545 of 1,220 Oregon clients in Q2 2026 (44.7%) lived outside Oregon (E4). They go home to integrate with no licensed support [mkt-01].

**The play:** Maya becomes the primary source for "after the session" numbers.

- Every data asset doubles as a lead magnet: people give an email to get results first, get the benchmark, or get the take-home kit.
- Every release rides a news date that is already on the calendar (§2).
- Reporters already cover every Oregon data release (E9). Give them the missing angle with a chart, a CSV and one quotable number, each pointing back to mayaallan.com.
- Google’s research surfaces (Dataset Search, Scholar, Data Studio) are live and free (E14–E16), and nobody in this niche uses them.

**Why one author can do this:** the raw public data is already published (E1–E5). The site already has four free tools, a 25-term glossary, a journal that facilitators are invited to share (E20), and 40 book scenarios. The only new primary data is one survey, and the tools themselves can host it.

## 7. Risks and red lines

- **Drug-use survey ethics and law.** Keep it anonymous. Ask nothing about dose or sourcing, and no location below state. Do not log IPs with answers. Adults 18+ only. Label it a practice survey, not research, and publish the methods and their limits. If a journal publication is wanted, get an IRB through an academic partner **before** collecting data.
- **Oregon rules.** Never ask centers for client-level data (E1).
- **Health claims.** Report what respondents said; make no efficacy or safety claims. Keep the "not therapy" line and the Fireside line on every page.
- **Journalist platforms.** One off-topic pitch can mean a permanent ban (E18).
- **Denominators.** 44.7% is the share of all clients served (E4). The market map’s 71% is the share of clients who gave a county. Always state which one is meant.
- **Overlap with Psychedelic Alpha (E13).** Differentiate on aftercare; do not copy their volume tracker.
- **Data terms.** OPS states no license (E1), so attribute it and imply no endorsement. DORA data is Public Domain (E5).
- **Link schemes.** Widget links use the brand anchor only, no paid links, and press releases go only on the site (no paid wires) [web-spam-redlines].
- **Paid search.** It is restricted for this topic [mkt-x-01]. Plan on none.
- **Capacity.** Moves M1–M14 add up to roughly 15 build days of code PRs plus about 1 hour a day of outreach. If time is short, the order is M9 → M4 → M1 → M2 → M6 → M7.
