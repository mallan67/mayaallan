## 2. Findings that change the plan (most important first)

1. **Colorado is almost empty on the client side.** The official *2025 DOR Annual Natural Medicine Report* (dated 2026-02-02, linked from nmd.colorado.gov) contains voluntary preliminary data. It shows **109 participants served in Aug–Dec 2025**: Aug 4, Sep 12, Oct 40, Nov 29, Dec 24. They were served in five counties (Boulder, Douglas, Eagle, Jefferson, Pitkin). By age: 21–29: 6; 30–39: 23; 40–49: 29; 50–59: 15; 60–69: 15; 70–79: 2; unknown: 19. The same report confirms 34 healing-center licenses issued (25 micro, 9 standard) from 52 applications. **Put the take-home kit into Oregon first.** Colorado client volume is about 2% of Oregon's.
2. **The Colorado facilitator numbers were misread.** On the official DPO table, the column holding 77 and 41 is labeled "Applications Received Pending Processing", and the "Applications Approved" column shows 7 and 2. The table was last updated 02/02/2026. The **live DORA open dataset** (`data.colorado.gov/resource/7s5z-vewr`, data updated 2026-09-24 10:35Z) shows these active licenses: **Facilitator 86**, **Clinical Facilitator 94**, **Facilitator in Training 691**, **Training Program 11**, Distinguished Educator 2. The dataset also gives each licensee's state (Facilitator: 49 in CO, 18 in OR, 9 in CA), which makes it the official, current B2B list for Colorado.
3. **The Oregon out-of-state share runs high.** In Q2-26 there were 1,220 clients; 291 opted out, leaving 929 respondents, and 51 of those did not answer the county question. 545 were from out of state (525 other US states, 20 outside the US), and 225 lived in the Oregon counties whose counts are published. About 108 more respondents fall in the 30 suppressed Oregon counties. The share is therefore **62.1% (545/878)**. **70.8% is an upper bound**, because it leaves the suppressed counties out. Q1-26: 48.8–57.6%. Say "about half to two-thirds", not "58–71%".
4. **Integration can happen outside the center.** ORS 475A.360(2) says "An integration session may be, but need not be, held at a psilocybin service center." Facilitators can therefore continue integration with clients after they go home. Centers sell integration themselves, so the kit has to **support** that service, not replace it, or centers will say no.
5. **MAPS now occupies the bulk channel.** The "MAPS Integration Workbook Bundle (20-Pack)" costs **$60** (about $3 a copy). It is aimed at "clinicians, therapists, and healing practitioners who want to share this support more broadly", and its product images were uploaded in 2026/07. The old MAPS Integration List (integration.maps.org) now redirects to the "Integration Station" resource page, so the provider list is **DEAD**. The whitespace in mkt-ws-04 and mkt-ws-01 is the *scenario casebook*, not "an integration workbook".
6. **The site does not yet have the pages the plan links to.** The live sitemap (19:21:01Z) has 38 URLs and exactly **one** scenario subpage, `/scenarios/ego-dissolution`. mkt-05, mkt-07 and mkt-ws-02 each need per-scenario pages built first.
7. **New Mexico timing conflicts.** The introduced SB 219 text reads "…and, by December 31, 2027, implement the program." KUNM (2026-03-17) says the program "will launch at the end of this year." The DOH page gives no date and says DOH "is now embarking on the critical task of developing the process for implementation." Treat end-2026 as UNVERIFIED.
8. **COMP360 has an extra gate.** Compass's Q2 release (EX-99.1, filed 2026-08-05) says "commercial launch expected in first half of 2027, subject to FDA approval and following Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) rescheduling". It notes that "approximately 90% of the U.S. patient population live in states that intend to reschedule within 30 days after Federal DEA" and that COMP360 would fit "more than 8,000 centers offering multi-hour treatments". At approval the focus is "REMS certification". It also says "almost 80% of the administration session [was] spent in silence", which supports the gap for a companion to read afterwards. Clinics will control which materials are handed out on site, so reach patients through search and through clinicians.

## 5. Policy and legal restrictions the map missed

1. **Google Ads, Healthcare and medicines policy** (support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031, 19:07:21Z): "Promotion of speculative and/or experimental medical treatments" is not allowed. This comes on top of the recreational-drugs rule the map already cited.
2. **Google Ads, restricted targeting** (answer/143465, 19:07:21Z): advertisers in sensitive-interest categories "are unable to use advertiser-curated audiences". Custom segments that point to sensitive landing pages "will only serve with Display campaigns to non-sensitive audiences or contextually". This rules out remarketing to site visitors.
3. **Google Ads recreational-drugs text** (answer/6014299, 19:07:11Z), reconfirmed verbatim: "Ads for products or services marketed as facilitating recreational drug use are not allowed. Ads for instructional content about producing, purchasing, or using recreational drugs are not allowed."
4. **PayPal Acceptable Use Policy** (paypal.com/us/legalhub/paypal/acceptableuse-full, 19:19:43Z) forbids transactions involving "(a) narcotics … (d) items that encourage, promote, facilitate or instruct others to engage in illegal activity". The on-site ebook uses PayPal, so microdosing tracks, retreat bundles and sourcing content must stay reflection-only.
5. **Oregon OAR 333-333-2400(2)** bars unsupported health claims. **Colorado 1 CCR 213-1** treats misleading advertising as a serious violation. Partner centers will vet every insert. Oregon facilitator conduct and financial-conflict rules are UNVERIFIED, so offer **no commissions** until they have been read.
6. **Medical-intent queries.** The "psilocybin therapy for dementia / alzheimer's / ptsd" autocompletes are treatment intents. A non-clinical book should not target them (health-claim risk under items 1 and 5).
7. **Email gating for UK circles** (London, Brighton) needs UK GDPR and PECR consent. US lists need CAN-SPAM compliance.
